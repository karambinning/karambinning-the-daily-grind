AboutThe Daily Grind

A café small enough to know its regulars by name, and old-fashioned enough to want to.

In short

Six tables, vinyl placemats, a wall of London Road photographs and a friendly counter run by Mandy.

  • Independent & locally owned

  • Vinyl-record placemats

  • Cooked-to-order kitchen

  • Closed Sundays

The Daily Grind doesn’t try to be a chain. It tries to be the café you remember from somewhere — small, warm, run by people who recognise you on the second visit and remember your order by the fifth. The room sits half a dozen tables; the kitchen is around the corner; the coffee machine is busy and the conversation is louder than the music.

The decoration is deliberate. The placemats are saved vinyl records — the kind you used to find in your dad’s loft — and the wall behind the counter is covered with historical photographs of London Road through the ages. Bakers, butchers, ironmongers, picture houses; lives that came before this one, hung at eye-level so that whoever sits down for a coffee can lift their head and notice them.

What gets cooked is what gets cooked when you order it. Baguettes — wholemeal or white — go in fresh. Soups are made in the kitchen, not warmed from a tin. Salads are put together with local ingredients on the day. None of it is fancy. All of it is honest.

Mandy runs the counter, and most of what regulars come back for is on her side of it: the friendly word, the genuine ask after the family, the cup of coffee that arrives the way you like it without you having to remind anyone. That’s a small café’s real menu, and it’s the one we work hardest on.
